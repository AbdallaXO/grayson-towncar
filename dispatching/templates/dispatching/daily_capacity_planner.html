How Scheduler Tuning Works
The Big Picture

When you click Auto-Assign or Schedule Builder, the scheduler looks at every unassigned job and every available driver, then asks: "Which driver is the best fit for this job?"

To answer that, it gives every driver-job combination a point total. The driver with the most points gets the job. These tuning settings control how many points each factor is worth.

Simple example: A job needs an SUV pickup at MCO at 1 PM.
  • Driver A has an SUV, just dropped off at MCO, and has 25 min free → gets lots of points
  • Driver B has a Sedan (wrong vehicle), is at Disney, and has 3 hours free → gets fewer points
Driver A wins and gets the job.
What Gets Scored (10 Factors)

Each driver-job pair gets points from these categories. They all add up to a final total:

FactorWhat It MeansTypical Points
Free Time (Buffer)How much time the driver has between their last job and this one30 to 120
Vehicle MatchDoes the driver's vehicle match what the customer booked?10 to 60
Vehicle RarityIs this the only driver with this vehicle type? Protect them for matching jobs15 to 80
LocationIs the driver already near the pickup? Or do they need to drive across town?0 to 50
Follow-Up WorkAfter this job, will the driver be near other upcoming jobs? (job chaining)0 to 45
Schedule FlowIs the driver already stacked with too many arrivals in a row?-40 to +30
Keep In-HouseReturns and cruise jobs get a bonus to stay with your drivers, not affiliates0 to 25
Job SpreadingDrivers with fewer jobs today get a slight edge, so work spreads evenly-15 to 0
Driver PreferencePrefer = soft bonus. Heavy = fill with preferred type first, then gaps. Only = strict filter (builder only)-10 to +40
Job PriceHigher-paying jobs get a small bonus (builder only)0 to 20
How Parameters Compete With Each Other

This is the most important thing to understand: every factor is competing for influence. If you turn one way up, it can overpower the others.

Example: Location set too high

If you set "Same Area" to 150, the scheduler will almost always pick the closest driver — even if that means:

  • Sending a Sprinter for a Sedan booking (vehicle mismatch doesn't matter enough to overcome 150 location points)
  • Ignoring that another driver has the perfect vehicle and perfect timing, but is 15 min away
Example: Good balance

With "Same Area" at 50 and "Vehicle Exact Match" at 60:

  • A driver with the right vehicle who's 15 min away (0 + 60 = 60) beats a driver with the wrong vehicle who's already there (50 + 25 = 75)... barely
  • But a driver with the right vehicle AND in the right area (50 + 60 = 110) clearly wins
  • This creates natural priorities: right vehicle + right location > right location alone > right vehicle alone
Example: Arrival stacking

If "3rd Arrival Penalty" is -40 but "Same Area" is 50, a driver at MCO who already has 2 arrivals still gets: 50 (location) - 40 (penalty) = 10 points from those two factors. They might still win.

To really prevent stacking, either make the penalty bigger (-70) or keep location moderate (40-50).

The Balancing Act

Think of each setting as a volume knob on a mixing board. Each one controls how loud that factor is in the final decision:

If you want the scheduler to care more about...
  • Keeping drivers local → turn up Location
  • Matching the right vehicle → turn up Vehicle Match + Rarity
  • Avoiding rushed schedules → turn up Perfect Free Time, turn down Tight/Risky
  • Maximizing jobs per driver → turn down Free Time scores, turn up Follow-Up Work
  • Mixing up job types → turn up Arrival Penalties + Streak Break
Watch out for...
  • Any single factor above 100 — it will dominate unless others are also high
  • Penalties weaker than bonuses — a -15 penalty can't overcome a +50 location bonus
  • Too many things turned up high — if everything is 100+, nothing stands out and results become unpredictable
  • Follow-Up Work vs Location — both try to position drivers well, but sometimes they disagree
How to Test & Find the Right Settings
  1. Save a snapshot first — Before changing anything, save your current schedule as a snapshot so you can compare or go back
  2. Change one thing at a time — If you change 5 settings at once and the schedule gets worse, you won't know which one caused it
  3. Use Reset Schedule — After changing settings, reset the schedule and re-run Auto-Assign to see the new results
  4. Compare against your snapshot — Look at what changed: Did returns stay in-house? Are drivers less stacked? Are vehicles matched better?
  5. Small changes first — Change by 10-20 points, not 50-100. Small tweaks can have big effects
  6. Reset to Defaults if lost — The defaults are a solid starting point. You can always go back

Auto-Assign vs Schedule Builder
Auto-Assign

Goes through each unassigned job one at a time (returns first, then arrivals) and picks the best available driver.

Uses: All the main settings (Free Time, Vehicle, Location, Rarity, Follow-Up, Flow, Keep In-House, Job Spreading)

Schedule Builder

For each driver, looks at ALL unassigned jobs and builds the best possible schedule. More thorough but takes longer.

Also uses: Builder-specific settings + Driver Preference + Job Price. Has its own separate Free Time and Flow settings.

Settings labeled "Builder:" only affect the Schedule Builder. All other settings affect Auto-Assign. Some factors (like Vehicle Match, Rarity, Location, Follow-Up) are shared by both.
