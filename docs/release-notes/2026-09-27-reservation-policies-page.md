# There is now a policies page on the website you can send a guest to

## Send this to the team

> Hey team — the website now has a Reservation Policies page: graysontowncar.com/policies
>
> It's written for guests and has every number from the field guide: 60 minutes included after landing then $100 an hour, 10 minutes at hotel pickups then $30 to hold 15 more, the 48-hour refund tiers, the 20-minute Publix stop with the overage, the $20 late-night fee, and the cleaning and car seat fees. It also says the Publix stop has to be booked in advance and pushes guests to pre-order for curbside pickup.
>
> When a guest asks "where does it say that?", send them the link. When you're offering the paid wait, the wording on the page matches what you're supposed to say on the call. Extra stop pricing is not on the page; that stays pricing, quoted by you.
>
> Nothing about how we actually charge or handle a wait has changed. The field guide is still the rule book; this page is the guest's copy of it.

---

## Behind the scenes

**Where it lives:** graysontowncar.com/policies, linked at the bottom of every page next to Terms of Service. The FAQ page's cancellation answer now points there too.

**Why:** The only place a guest could read the wait time and no-show rules was the confirmation email, and it only showed them on some trips. Disputes need a public, dated page to point at. This is the first step; the next one puts a short "Good to know" version in every email and text.

**Expect to be asked:**
- "Does it say same-day Publix is a no?" It says same-day usually can't be fitted in, and when it can it's priced as an additional stop. That matches the field guide's Tier S3 rule.
- "What about cruise terminal pickups?" The page groups them under "all other pickups" with the 10-minute rule. If we want a different number for the port, it's a one-line change.
- "Can I send the link by text?" Yes, it's short and reads fine on a phone.
