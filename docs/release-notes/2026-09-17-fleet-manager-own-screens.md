# The fleet screens are his own, and only offer cars he can actually get to

## Send this to the team

> Hey team — the fleet screens have had a clean-up, and they now only point at
> cars that are genuinely standing still while the fleet manager is at work.
>
> 1. **Inspections** used to suggest a car by saying "Free 7:04 PM – 11:04 PM".
>    He finishes at four, so that was no use. It now only counts time between
>    7:30 AM and 4:00 PM, and it works out whether there is really enough of it —
>    twenty minutes to walk the car, plus getting it into HQ and back out. A car
>    that is out on the road all day says so instead of quoting an evening.
> 2. **The day** marks those same gaps in gold, so a car free for an hour and
>    three quarters at half one in the afternoon is now marked, and a three-hour
>    gap at eight in the evening is not.
> 3. That board also lists the cars in number order — 001, 002, 003 — every
>    morning, so a car is always in the same place. Shop cars stay in the list,
>    marked in red, instead of dropping to the bottom.
> 4. Hover any block on it and the trip details come up straight away. No more
>    waiting a second for the little grey box.
> 5. On the **Desk**, tap a fault code and the plain-English note underneath now
>    says what it is: something we wrote, not a mechanic's diagnosis, on a system
>    still being built. Ring the shop before spending money on the strength of
>    one. The code itself and the car's own wording are unchanged.
>
> His working hours are set on his account, so if they ever change, both screens
> follow.
>
> Nothing about dispatch changed — the board, reservations, quoting and pay are
> all exactly where they were, and every fleet screen is still open to any of you.

---

## Behind the scenes

**Where it lives:** The Fleet screens — the Desk, The day, and Inspections.

**Why:** He walks the cars himself, at HQ, on a 7:30–4 shift, and both screens
were pointing him at cars that only stood still after he'd gone home. Two things
were wrong. They counted the whole 24 hours, and they measured an inspection
against the cost of a garage visit — 90 minutes of service plus 30 to get the car
to the bay and back. On a normal Thursday that made 17 of 18 cars look
unreachable when they had clear hour-long gaps at ten in the morning.

The figure it uses now comes from the real example: #008 runs Disney → MCO at
eight, clears MCO by half past, is at HQ by quarter to nine, is walked by twenty
past, and makes a 9:40 arrival. So an inspection is twenty minutes, the trip to
HQ and back out is about half an hour, and a hole has to be about fifty minutes
before it is worth anything. No mechanical work happens at HQ, so none of the
shop's numbers apply to it — booking a car in for real shop work is still the
Outlook's job, with its own longer window.

His top bar also said "Dispatching" and, if he clicked it, put him on the
marketing site with a menu offering Commissions, Driver Pay and Pay Rates. That
menu only ever checked whether someone was staff. It now shows him his own six
pages.

**Expect to be asked:**
- *"Why does it only suggest three or four cars some days?"* — because that is
  how many are genuinely reachable. It won't pad the list with cars he can't get
  to. The count of cars only free after he leaves is printed underneath.
- *"It skipped a 45-minute gap."* — deliberately. Twenty minutes of walking plus
  the round trip to HQ does not fit in forty-five.
- *"Does that warning mean the codes are unreliable?"* — no. The code and the
  car's own description come straight off the vehicle. It is the sentence we
  wrote underneath, explaining what the code means for a car with guests in it,
  that is ours and could be wrong.
- *"His hours changed / someone else is covering."* — the hours are on the
  person's own account, not the company's, so a second fleet hand on an evening
  shift would correctly be offered the evening windows.
- *"I opened Fleet and the tabs moved."* — for dispatchers and founders nothing
  moved. The second row of tabs is only hidden for him, because he already
  carries those six links along the top.

**The one soft number:** the half hour for the HQ round trip is a fleet-wide
average. HQ is twelve minutes from MCO and that is where most of the dead time
sits, but nothing in the system knows where a given car actually is when its gap
opens — one dropping at a Disney resort is further out. If he finds it is sending
him to cars he can't reach in time, that number is the one to raise.
